every state, not just the happy path

from problem to decision

remi was originally a problem I had, before it was sketches or wireframes. I’ve used photo-cleaning apps before, and none of them felt trustworthy or smooth. I felt like a cash cow for pop-up advertisements. I started sketching out ideas and rambling to myself in my notes app on the train. when I got home, I put my notes into AI and had it help me come up with some preliminary ideas. then I launched adobe illustrator and wireframed every screen, like I would’ve done before AI. that gave me a reliable backbone, made by me and me only.

I wanted a clickable version because you don’t know how the user flow will go until you’ve experienced it yourself. the flow wasn’t as smooth as it looked in the flats, so I reworked parts of the prototype until it led smoothly from A to B. try remi →

checking the output

AI tends to hallucinate unless you prompt it directly to fix an issue. since we were working with data, I didn’t want anything misconstrued to the public, so I went through every screen and picked apart what wasn’t the most honest way to display information.

I never use auto AI website builders that make the product from A to Z. my process is formed by my fine arts discipline and data education, and once I have a project 60% built out with my own tools, that’s when I start working with AI to bring it to the next level. and finicky attention to detail: claude doesn’t open the laptop at 4am because one of the buttons is overlapping something it shouldn’t.

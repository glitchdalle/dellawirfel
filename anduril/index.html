at UPMC: one front door

a team’s reports, all in one place.

as a quality analytics intern on the HEDIS production team, I designed and developed an excel-connected power BI homepage that organizes report navigation and makes the team’s resources easier to reach.

I grouped all the sources, their breakdowns and their titles in an excel sheet, then linked each one to a clickable button in power BI. the team responded overwhelmingly positively and appreciated the new resource, acknowledging they’d be using it long after my summer internship ended.

I also wrote documentation the team adopted for its azure DevOps migration.

NYC 311, in layers

the 311 project turns five years of complaints into one skyline: a line for every zip code, drawn for heat, unsanitary conditions, rodents and mold. what I decided about hierarchy:

  1. 01one story at a time

    my original tableau workbook was static. now the story unfolds part by part as you scroll, so it stays bite-sized.

  2. 02labels where they matter

    the three tallest lines show their zip code for every complaint type. hover any line for the rest of its numbers.

  3. 03one shared scale

    the all-four view had given each type its own scale, making 47 mold complaints look as tall as 37,141 heat complaints. now one shared scale keeps every line comparable.

and I wrote down what the data can’t say. 311 relies on people to report problems, so not every issue gets recorded, and a neighborhood can look better than it is. see the 311 project →

remi, one workflow start to finish

remi began as a problem I had, then notes on the train, sketches, adobe illustrator wireframes for every screen, and a clickable prototype. I wanted a clickable version because you don’t know how the user flow will go until you’ve experienced it yourself. the flow wasn’t as smooth as it looked in the flats, so I reworked parts of it until it led smoothly from A to B. engineering hasn’t happened yet: next, I would develop remi in swiftUI for the app store. try remi →

learning unfamiliar things

for my CUNY dissertation project I trained a topic model on 16,738 abstracts. what surprised me was the artifacts I had to remove, like lcub and rcub for left and right curly brackets, which were frequent enough to skew the results. I let the model find patterns instead of deciding which words to look for, and I wrote up what it can’t tell you. see the project →

at FIT I presented wireframes, UI/UX and product designs on 11x17 decks: moodboards, preliminary sketches, a first round of design, then refinements. then we sat in critique for hours so people could say how to improve the work, and I took it.

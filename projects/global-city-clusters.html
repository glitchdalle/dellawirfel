global cities, grouped by k-means

typeclustering & mapping
year—
toolsk-means (k = 5), tableau
roleanalyst & designer

summary

354 big cities, sorted into five groups by an algorithm instead of by country.

question

if you set aside borders and let an algorithm group the world's big cities by where they sit and what they're like, which cities end up together? k-means sorted 354 cities into five clusters using their population, elevation, latitude and longitude.

five clusters, one world

each circle is one of 354 large cities, sized by population. an algorithm sorted them into five groups. scroll to light up one group at a time.

    from the global city clusters map and the two median bar charts in my tableau workbook. hover a city for its numbers.

    interactive — hover and click to explore

    reading the clusters

    the groups come out partly geographic and partly about size and altitude. some clusters are regions (an eastern, mostly asian group and a western and northern group with NYC, london and cairo), one is defined by sheer size (the megacities) and one by height alone, pulling together cities as far apart as bogotá, nairobi, tehran and kunming.

    limitations

    k-means needs the number of groups chosen up front, and five was my choice. it also leans on whichever measures vary most, so the scaling of population, elevation and coordinates shapes the result. the clusters describe patterns in these four numbers, not culture, economy or history.

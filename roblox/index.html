hello

hello. I am della. I like to utilize design and data to solve problems and spread information. I design after watching what people do and what they need to do it.

why roblox

I’ve played roblox extensively. I’m the house party host on work at a pizza place, if that shows anything. I see how roblox has created a blueprint for multiple user-developed mini games hosted on one site. it’s a beautiful place to polish skills even on your own, I can’t imagine how helpful it would be to work with the team that helped make that happen.

remi, a problem I had

photo-cleaning apps never felt trustworthy or smooth to me, and I felt like a cash cow for pop-up ads. I sketched ideas in my notes app on the train, then wireframed every screen in illustrator. I wanted a clickable version because you don’t know how a flow will go until you’ve experienced it yourself. it wasn’t as smooth as it looked in the flats, so I reworked parts of it until it led smoothly from A to B. try remi →

design beyond screens

for remi’s look I went after beauty brands like rhode instead of the usual utility apps: big photos, lots of space, your library sorted by season like winter 2024.

outside the screen, I minored in art history, I take commissioned painting, and I hand-drew the stage background for “timeless.” while designing layouts for the new york public library in school, I swapped every visual on the page for its opposite: dark became light, large became small. it showed me the trap the design had been sitting in and gave it a fresh new life.

for everyone

the remi prototype turns off its slow photo motion for anyone who has reduced motion switched on, and every image on this page has alt text.

how I work

the very first thing I do is open procreate on my iPad and thumbnail-sketch in 2B pencil. I share my first sketches, then share again once wireframes and layout blocking are down, and I always show a friend or colleague, because one pair of eyes staring at anything gets confused fast.

at FIT I presented wireframes, UI/UX and product designs on 11x17 decks: moodboards, preliminary sketches, a first round of design, then refinements. then we sat in critique for hours so people could say how to make the work better. brutal art critiques taught me how to take advice.

I know figma from real work. at joanna vargas the company computers only had figma, so that’s where I designed the original spa girl digest, before moving it to illustrator. for code I use claude code and cursor: once I have a project about 60% built with my own tools, that’s when I start working with AI to take it further.
